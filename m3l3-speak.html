1

🎯 Deliver bad news — the 2-week delay

60 seconds · the client call you don't want to make

The scene. You're the project lead. The new ordering system was supposed to go live on the 15th — your client's biggest launch of the year depends on it. Testing found a data risk you can't ship with. You have to call the client today and tell them: the project will be 2 weeks late. They are not going to be happy. Your job: say it straight, hold the line, and keep the relationship.

Prepare — your chunk plan

  • Open with the preface — #1 I need to be honest with you: (or #2) — it softens the blow before the news lands.
  • Deliver the fact fast: the date, the 2 weeks. Don't bury it in small talk.
  • Say no cleanly — #5 I'm afraid that's not possible. One "sorry", not five. No over-apologizing.
  • Pivot immediately to what you CAN do: #6 What I can do is… — own the fix.

📖 Model answer — 60 seconds

“I need to be honest with you: this isn't the update I wanted to give you. The new ordering system won't go live on the 15th — we're looking at a two-week delay. I know this affects your launch plans, and I take that seriously. I'm afraid that's not possible to shorten the testing phase; we've seen what happens when we skip it, and I won't risk your data on day one. What I can do is move your team to the front of the support queue, send you a daily progress report, and confirm the new go-live date in writing by Friday. I'll own this personally until we're live.”

🎯 Disagree upward — the plan that will fail

60 seconds · your director's meeting

The scene. Your director just presented her plan to the team: launch the new service in three markets at once, next month. Everyone is nodding. You ran the numbers last night — support is already at capacity, and a triple launch will break response times. You believe the plan will fail. You also know you can't afford to burn this bridge. Your job: disagree with your boss, in the room, without becoming the problem.

Prepare — your chunk plan

  • Validate first — #3 I see where you're coming from, but… or #4 That's a fair point, but…
  • Attack the plan, never the person. "The plan risks X" beats "You're wrong."
  • Keep hard chunks OUT of upward conversations — never "as far as I'm concerned" to your director.
  • Close by co-owning the solution: #10 How can we make this work?

📖 Model answer — 60 seconds

“I see where you're coming from, but I need to share a concern before we commit. Launching in three markets at once would give us huge momentum — that's a fair point, but our support team is already at capacity. If we go live everywhere on day one, response times will drop, and fast response is the one thing our best clients praise us for. I know the board wants speed, and I respect that — but speed that breaks service will cost us more than waiting. How can we make this work? What if we pilot in one market, prove the model, then roll out the other two with the lessons learned? You'd get the win you want — just sequenced.”

🎯 The furious client call — capstone

90 seconds · the billing error your team made

The scene. The phone rings. It's your biggest client — and he is furious. Your team double-charged his March renewal and added a late fee on top of their own mistake. He's talking about leaving. Your job: take the full 90 seconds, use at least 5 chunks — validate (#7), reset (#8), one clean no (#5), an alternative (#6), and a proposal (#9) — and turn this call into the reason he stays.

Prepare — your chunk plan

  • Validate before you redirect — #7 I completely understand your frustration. An angry client who feels heard calms down.
  • Reset the temperature: #8 Let's take a step back.
  • Own the error first — your team made it — then say one clean no: #5 I'm afraid that's not possible. No caving, no groveling.
  • Pivot and propose: #6 What I can do is… + #9 Here's what I propose: — end with a plan, not an apology.

📖 Model answer — 90 seconds

“I completely understand your frustration. A billing error on an invoice this size would upset anyone — you have every right to call. Let's take a step back. I've pulled up your account, and I can see exactly what happened: our system charged you twice for the March renewal. You're right, and I'm sorry — that one's on us. Now, I'm afraid that's not possible to reverse the duplicate charge today itself; finance needs one business day to process it. But what I can do is flag it as urgent personally, email you the correction confirmation tonight, and credit the late fee our error caused. Here's what I propose: we fix the invoice, I put a double-check on your account so this never happens again, and I call you tomorrow to confirm the refund reached you. Fair?”

#7 validate #8 reset #5 clean no #6 alternative #9 proposal
