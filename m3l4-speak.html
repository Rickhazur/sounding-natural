1

🎯 The Opener — break the ice

60 seconds · conference coffee break

The scene. You're at the pre-conference coffee break. A stranger stands alone by the coffee table, name badge half-hidden. Nobody has talked to her yet. Your job: walk up, break the ice, find common ground, and leave her glad you came over. Use chunks #1–#4.

Prepare — your chunk plan

  • Open light, never loaded — #1 How's your week going? is warm and pressure-free; it invites a real answer.
  • Invite their story — #2 What brings you here? beats any yes/no question.
  • Find shared ground — #3 Did you come far? and #4 How long have you been at…? — travel and tenure are universal, never intrusive.
  • Your goal is common ground, not their life story. Two minutes of warmth beats ten minutes of questions.

📖 Model answer — 60 seconds

“Excuse me — How's your week going? I hope the coffee's better than yesterday's. I'm Ricardo — I'm on the digital side. What brings you here? Are you following the marketing track or the product one? Did you come far? I flew in from Bogotá, so the jet lag and I are best friends this week. And How long have you been at…? — sorry, I didn't catch where you work! Ah — fantastic. Which session are you most excited about this afternoon?”

#1 icebreaker #2 invitation #3 travel talk #4 tenure

🎯 Keep It Flowing — genuine interest

60 seconds · the conversation is rolling

The scene. The ice is broken. Your new acquaintance, Roberto, just mentioned he left marine biology to become a UX designer. The conversation has oxygen — your job: keep it flowing with react-before-ask and open questions. Use chunks #5–#6.

Prepare — your chunk plan

  • React BEFORE you ask — #5 That sounds fascinating — how did you get into it? The reaction proves you were listening; the question invites his story.
  • Never fire a cold question — reaction first, curiosity second. Questions without reaction feel like interrogations.
  • Go deeper with #6 What do you enjoy most about…? — passion questions beat fact questions.
  • Chase THEIR interesting detail — don't change the subject to you.

📖 Model answer — 60 seconds

“Wait — marine biology to UX design? That sounds fascinating — how did you get into it? I mean, from studying whales to studying users — there has to be a story there. And What do you enjoy most about…? — sorry, about the design work — there must be a favorite part that makes the career switch worth it. See, that's what I love about these mixers: everyone has a story behind the job title. What's the one thing from the ocean that you still miss?”

#5 react-before-ask #6 passion question

🎯 The Full Encounter — capstone

90 seconds · warm intro → common ground → graceful exit

The scene. Last coffee break of the day. By the window stands Priya — your client's account lead, someone you've emailed for months but never met. Your job: the full encounter in 90 seconds — a warm intro, common ground, genuine interest, and a graceful three-part exit: signal (#9) → positive close → future bridge (#10). Use chunks across all five moments.

Prepare — your chunk plan

  • Intro with warmth — #7 It's great to finally meet you in person. or #8 I've been looking forward to meeting you. Name the moment.
  • Common ground — #2 What brings you here?, #3 Did you come far?, #4 How long have you been at…?
  • Keep it flowing — #5 That sounds fascinating — how did you get into it? (react-before-ask) or #6.
  • Exit in three parts: signal (#9 I won't keep you any longer —) + positive close + bridge (#10 Let's grab a coffee sometime.). Never vanish mid-conversation, never hold them hostage.

📖 Model answer — 90 seconds

“Hi, I'm Ricardo — It's great to finally meet you in person. We've been on the same session mailing list for months, and I always wanted to put a face to the name. What brings you here? Which track caught your eye? Did you come far? And How long have you been at…? — right, of course, the fintech world. That sounds fascinating — how did you get into it? Product to client success is a rare move. Honestly, I've been looking forward to meeting you. This has been great — but I won't keep you any longer — I can see the next session is about to start. Let's grab a coffee sometime. I'd love to hear how that launch turns out.”

#7 warmth #8 anticipation #9 exit signal #10 bridge
